Prelude

America began as a line on the asphalt. Straight, distant, unreal. One meter above the ground the horizon was everywhere and the Moto Guzzi, loaded with road and dreams, moved forward like a ship on a solid sea. Behind me Anna, ahead eight thousand kilometers: from New York to Los Angeles.

That journey came from an old idea, a coast‑to‑coast imagined as teenagers and kept in a drawer for years. Then life moved on: the right meeting, the right bike, the right time. We decided to cross the United States on our own, without fixed stages, avoiding cities, choosing back roads, sleeping wherever it happened. We didn’t want to “see” America, we wanted to be inside it.

The road tested us right away: a flat tire, violent storms, suffocating heat, long stretches without services. Then Route 66, with its ghost towns and abandoned pumps, the Mississippi crossed like a symbolic border, meetings with real, curious, often generous people. The farther west we went, the faster the landscape changed: plains, deserts, canyons, red mountains, wind‑beaten plateaus.

We entered the great West leaving certainties behind. Monument Valley in the rain, dirt roads on the edge of emptiness, absolute silence. Then the Grand Canyon, Zion, Bryce: places you don’t cross, you endure. Finally Death Valley, faced at the worst hour, with heat burning the skin and air stealing breath. The bike held up, so did we.

When we started descending toward California we realized the journey was already becoming memory. It wasn’t a vacation, but a crossing: of geography, of limits, and of a moment in our lives that would not return. This is the story of that road.
